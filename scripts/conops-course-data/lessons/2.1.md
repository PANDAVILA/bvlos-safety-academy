## What you will learn

- The three layers of the operational volume and how they nest
- How to size the ground risk buffer
- How the volumes map to the FAA ground risk buffer concept

## The three layers

- **Flight geography (FG)**: the planned flight path with the tolerance you can really fly. It includes navigation accuracy and the corridor width, not just the centre line.
- **Contingency volume (CV)**: the space around the FG that the UA may enter while you apply contingency procedures, such as a pilot-commanded return or a manual recovery. Its size comes from the UA's response time and speed.
- **Ground risk buffer (GRB)**: the ground footprint beyond the CV where a UA that has lost control could come down.

Together, FG and CV are the **operational volume**. The GRB sits on the ground below and around it.

## Sizing the volumes

1. **FG**: planned route plus navigation error. If the UA position error is 10 m and the corridor tolerance is 20 m, the FG half-width is at least 30 m.
2. **CV**: how far the UA travels from the moment a problem occurs until the contingency procedure takes effect. A UA flying at 25 m/s with a 6 second reaction time travels 150 m.
3. **GRB**: for a fly-away, the distance the UA could travel before termination. For a ballistic descent, the distance from height and speed. A common starting point is a **1:1 ratio** between flight height and horizontal distance: at 120 m height, a 120 m buffer, then adjusted for speed, wind and failure mode.

## Corridor width

For a linear route the total ground footprint across the corridor is:

**width = 2 x (FG half-width + CV + GRB)**

With a 30 m FG half-width, a 150 m CV and a 120 m GRB, the half-width is 300 m and the footprint is 600 m wide. Population density must be checked inside this whole footprint.

## FAA terminology

The FAA waiver guidance refers to a **ground risk buffer** with a common starting point of at least a 1:1 relationship between altitude and horizontal distance from people, adjusted for speed and glide distance. Your ConOps should describe it with numbers and show how each value was derived.
