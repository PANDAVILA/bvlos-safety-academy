## What you will learn

- The standard structure of a BVLOS ConOps

## What a ConOps is

A **Concept of Operations** describes, in plain language, what you intend to do, where, with what, with whom and under which limits. It is the first document in the chain, and every later document refers to it: the risk assessment takes its inputs from it and the Operations Manual implements it.

It is **not** a marketing brochure and **not** the manual. It does not contain checklists. It contains decisions.

## Who reads it

- **The authority**: does the described operation match the approval requested, and are the limits clear?
- **The client**: will this operation deliver the service, within the site rules?
- **The insurer**: what can go wrong, and what controls exist?
- **The crew**: what is the mission and what is out of scope?
- **Air traffic and other airspace users**: where and when will the UAS be flying?

## A structure that works

1. Purpose and scope
2. Mission description and use cases
3. Operating environment: terrain, population, airspace, weather, obstacles, radio
4. Operational volume and routes
5. UAS description and performance
6. C2 link and navigation
7. Detect and avoid and airspace integration
8. Crew, roles and training
9. Normal procedures (summary)
10. Contingency and emergency procedures (summary)
11. Risk assessment summary and residual risk
12. Limits, assumptions and change control

<div class="callout"><strong>Rule of thumb:</strong> if a sentence in the ConOps cannot be traced to a decision, a number or a limit, cut it. Reviewers read ConOps documents closely, so vague text costs you credibility.</div>
