## What you will learn

- The crew roles in a BVLOS operation
- How to define competence and training
- How roles map to the proposed FAA Part 108 positions

## Roles

- **Remote pilot in command (RPIC)**: responsible for the safe conduct of the flight, with authority to stop or abort
- **Remote pilot or UAS operator at the control station**: flies or monitors the UA
- **Payload operator**: operates sensors or release systems
- **Visual or airspace observers**: detect traffic and report
- **Ground crew**: launch and recovery, site control, safety of people on site
- **Mission or operations manager**: plans, approves and holds the operation within the manual's limits
- **Safety manager**: audits, investigates and reviews occurrences

The proposed Part 108 uses two roles that map to these: an **operations supervisor**, responsible for overall safety, regulatory compliance and personnel training, and a **flight coordinator**, who directly oversees flights and can intervene.

## Competence

Define what each role must know and be able to do, and how you check it:

- **Knowledge**: regulations, airspace, meteorology, the aircraft systems, the ConOps and the manual
- **Skills**: normal and abnormal handling, lost-link and fly-away response, simulator or flight checks
- **Behaviours**: crew resource management, communication, workload management, assertiveness
- **Currency**: recent experience and recurrent training

Keep records: who trained, when, on what, by whom and the result.

## Crew size and workload

A BVLOS crew is sized by tasks, not by habit. List the tasks per phase and check that one person is never required to do two incompatible tasks at the same time, such as monitoring traffic and handling a payload anomaly.

## Fatigue and duty

Set maximum duty periods, breaks and handover rules for long corridor missions. A tired observer is a failed barrier.
