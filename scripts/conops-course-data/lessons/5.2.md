## What you will learn

- The structure of an Operations Manual for a BVLOS operation
- How to keep the manual, the ConOps and the risk assessment aligned
- How to manage changes and records

## Structure of an Operations Manual

1. **Document control**: owner, version, approval, distribution
2. **General**: purpose, scope, definitions, references
3. **Organization and responsibilities**: roles, authority, competence and training
4. **The UAS**: description, limits, configuration, maintenance and inspection
5. **Operating limits**: weather, performance, volumes, altitudes, airspace conditions
6. **Normal procedures and checklists**
7. **Contingency and emergency procedures**
8. **Detect and avoid, airspace and coordination procedures**
9. **Security, data and cyber**
10. **Occurrence reporting and records**
11. **Safety management**: hazard reporting, risk assessment and review
12. **Appendices**: forms, maps, contact lists, ELA sheets

## Keep three documents aligned

| ConOps says | Risk assessment says | Manual says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fly at 80 to 120 m AGL | Highest point of volume 120 m AGL | Operational ceiling 120 m AGL |
| Airspace observers at 4 km spacing | M1(C) or TMPR depends on observers | Observer procedure and placement table |
| Parachute fitted | M2 credit | Inspection and repack schedule |

Whenever one changes, check the other two. Add a column to each mitigation in the risk assessment that says **where** in the manual it is implemented.

## Change control and records

- Use a revision list and an effective date
- Review after every occurrence, every aircraft or software change and at least annually
- Keep training, maintenance, flight and occurrence records for the period your authority and your SMS require
- Control who may change the manual, and make sure the crew uses the current version in the field

## Approval and use

Authorities differ: some approve the manual with the authorization, others review it. Even where it is not approved, a clear and consistent manual is evidence that you can run the operation as promised.

<div class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> test the manual. Ask someone who did not write it to run a mission brief from it. Every question they ask shows a gap.</div>
