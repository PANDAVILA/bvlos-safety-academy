## C2 architectures

- **Direct radio line of sight**: simple and independent of networks, but limited by terrain and range
- **Cellular (LTE or 5G)**: long range where coverage exists, but coverage, latency and handovers vary with height and location
- **Satellite**: global coverage, higher latency and cost
- **Mixed or redundant links**: a primary link plus an independent backup with a defined switch-over

Describe the **lost-link behaviour** too: what the UA does after how many seconds, and what the crew does.

## A simple link budget

Free-space path loss in dB is:

**FSPL = 20 log10(d in km) + 20 log10(f in MHz) + 32.44**

For 5 km at 2,400 MHz: 20 log10(5) is about 14.0, 20 log10(2,400) is about 67.6, so FSPL is about **114 dB**.

Then:

**received power = transmit power + antenna gains - path loss - other losses**

With 30 dBm transmit power, 3 dBi and 8 dBi antennas and 3 dB of other losses: 30 + 3 + 8 - 114 - 3 = **-76 dBm**. If the receiver needs -95 dBm, the **fade margin** is 19 dB. Many designers look for a margin of at least 10 to 20 dB, but define your own criterion and justify it.

## Spectrum

- Use frequencies and equipment authorized for the use. In FAA waiver guidance, links for safety-critical BVLOS functions should not rely on devices that operate on an interference basis.
- Check coverage maps and measure along the route, including at height.
- Plan for interference and test for it.

## Security and integrity

Authenticate and encrypt the C2 link, control who can command the UA and keep software and configuration under change control.
