## What you will learn

- How to design routes and corridors for a BVLOS mission
- How to choose launch, landing and emergency landing areas
- How to choose altitudes and reference heights

## Route design principles

1. **Keep it simple**: fewer turns, fewer waypoints and shorter exposure over populated areas.
2. **Fly where the risk is low**: follow the least populated side of a road or railway, avoid built-up areas and gatherings.
3. **Respect obstacle clearance**: set the minimum height above the highest obstacle in the corridor plus a margin, and check that wires and masts are in the database.
4. **Design for the contingency**: every leg should have a reachable safe recovery option.
5. **Check the whole volume**, not only the route line.

## Altitude and height

State the **reference** clearly. Above ground level (AGL), above mean sea level (AMSL) and height above the take-off point are different. Over variable terrain, a constant AGL needs terrain following, and a constant AMSL changes the height above people. Make the vertical limits explicit, such as "operational ceiling 120 m AGL".

## Launch, landing and emergency areas

- **Launch and landing sites**: access control, wind exposure, obstacles, clearances and the ability to keep uninvolved people away
- **Emergency landing areas (ELAs)**: pre-surveyed open areas with no people, along the route and within reach of the UA's glide or descent. For long routes, set the spacing so the UA can always reach one.
- **Alternates**: for aircraft with endurance limits, a second landing site for weather or battery reserve

## Reserve and endurance

Plan the energy for the route, the return or alternate, and a safety reserve. Record the minimum energy decision points along the route.

## Documents to produce

- A route map with FG, CV, GRB and population density
- A table of waypoints, heights and legs
- A list of launch, landing and ELA sites with coordinates and photos
- An energy plan with go/no-go values

<div class="callout"><strong>Example.</strong> A fixed wing at 120 m AGL with a glide ratio of 20:1 can glide about 2.4 km. If ELAs are 4 km apart, no point on the route is more than 2 km from one, which leaves margin for wind. On a 42 km corridor that means about 11 sites, and more where terrain or obstacles limit the glide.</div>
