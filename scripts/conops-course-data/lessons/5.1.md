## What you will learn

- The difference between contingency and emergency procedures
- The core failure cases to write procedures for
- Reporting duties and how to link the ERP to your safety management system

## Contingency and emergency

- A **contingency** is a foreseen abnormal event that the operation can handle inside the contingency volume, such as a degraded link or a low battery warning. The aim is to return to normal operations or land safely.
- An **emergency** is a situation where there is an immediate threat to people or property, such as a loss of control or a fire. The aim is to protect people first.

Write each procedure with a **trigger**, a **first action**, a **decision owner**, a **time limit** and an **end state**.

## Failure cases to cover

| Case | Typical elements |
| --- | --- |
| Lost C2 link | Time before failsafe, programmed behaviour, route to a safe area, link recovery attempts |
| GNSS loss or degradation | Alternative navigation, return rules, geofence behaviour |
| Detect-and-avoid alert | Avoidance manoeuvre, notification, resumption criteria |
| Low battery or fuel | Decision points, nearest ELA, minimum reserves |
| Propulsion or control failure | Descent mode, parachute or termination, area to avoid |
| Fly-away | Last known position, tracking, notification of air traffic, termination option |
| Battery fire | People away and upwind, follow manufacturer guidance, call the fire service |
| Weather deterioration | Limits, abort rules, alternate sites |

## Reporting

- Preserve logs, memory cards and wreckage after an event.
- In the US, accidents are reported to the NTSB under 49 CFR Part 830, and Part 107 pilots report certain injuries or property damage to the FAA within 10 calendar days (§107.9).
- In the EU, reportable occurrences must be notified within 72 hours of becoming aware. For example, Spain's AESA states this 72-hour deadline, and accidents and serious incidents also go to the accident investigation authority.
- Your authorization may add its own reporting conditions.

## Link to your SMS

Report, investigate, learn. Feed occurrences into your hazard log and update the risk assessment and the manual. Run drills: a tabletop exercise about twice a year and a live exercise annually are common practice.

<div class="callout"><strong>Do not improvise.</strong> The best time to decide what to do about a fly-away is before the flight, with the crew and the data.</div>
