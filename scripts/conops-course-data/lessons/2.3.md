## What you will learn

- How airspace class and local restrictions affect a BVLOS plan
- What coordination and notification you may need
- How electronic conspicuity and remote identification fit in

## Know the airspace

Before you finalise the route, collect:

- **Airspace class** along the whole volume, including the vertical limits
- **Restricted, danger and prohibited areas**, temporary restrictions and UAS geographical zones
- **Airfields and helipads** near the corridor, their traffic patterns and hours
- **Low-level traffic**: helicopters, aerial work, agricultural aircraft, paragliders and gliders
- **Special airspace**: military low-flying areas, U-space or UTM zones

## Coordination options

- **Air traffic service coordination**: required in controlled airspace and often useful near airfields. Agree on who calls whom, on which frequency or channel, and what happens during contingencies.
- **NOTAM or equivalent notification**: informs other airspace users. It does not separate traffic.
- **Letters of agreement** with airfield operators, military units or other airspace users
- **Segregated airspace**: a temporary restricted area, a temporary segregated area or an active restricted area can make traffic encounters unlikely
- **Outreach**: local flying clubs, helicopter operators and farmers

## Identification and visibility

- In the United States, many UAS must broadcast **Remote ID**. In the EU, direct remote identification and geo-awareness requirements apply depending on class and category.
- Anti-collision lighting and conspicuity measures help crewed pilots see the UA.
- Cooperative electronic conspicuity, such as ADS-B In on the UA or a ground receiver, helps you see them.

## Document the airspace argument

Show in the ConOps what you found, what coordination you agreed and what residual traffic risk remains. This is the evidence for the strategic mitigations you claim in the risk assessment.

<div class="callout"><strong>Check locally.</strong> Airspace rules, remote identification classes and notification procedures differ by country and change. Confirm what applies to your operation with the competent authority.</div>
