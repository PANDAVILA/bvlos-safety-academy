## What you will learn

- The phases of a BVLOS mission and the procedure for each
- How to write a go/no-go decision
- How to write checklists that crews actually use

## The phases

1. **Planning**: mission brief, route and volume check, NOTAMs and airspace status, weather, C2 coverage, aircraft and crew readiness
2. **Site setup**: site control, equipment layout, observers in position, communications checks
3. **Pre-flight**: aircraft inspection, software and configuration, battery or fuel state, link test, failsafe verification
4. **Launch and climb**: clearances, takeoff checks, first-leg monitoring
5. **En route**: monitoring, reporting, handovers between control stations or crews
6. **Approach and landing**: landing site status, energy check, go-around criteria
7. **Post-flight**: inspection, data, defect reports, debrief

## Go/no-go

Write the criteria as numbers where you can:

- Wind and gusts below the limit for the aircraft and for the ELAs
- Visibility and ceiling above the minimum
- C2 link quality above the threshold on the test
- Battery or fuel with the required reserve
- Airspace status confirmed, no conflicting activity
- Crew fit and within duty limits
- No unresolved defects

If any item fails, the answer is no-go. Give the RPIC the authority to say it and a way to record it.

## Briefing and handovers

A short structured brief before each flight reduces errors: mission, route, limits, roles, contingencies, communication plan. When control passes from one person to another, use a defined handover with a read-back.

## Checklists

- Keep them short and in the order of work
- Use challenge and response for critical items
- Separate normal, abnormal and emergency checklists
- Test them with the crew and update them after each lesson learnt

<div class="callout"><strong>Example.</strong> "Proceed only if sustained wind is below 10 m/s, gusts below 14 m/s, C2 margin is above 15 dB on the test, and battery reserve at the point of no return is above 25%." Your own numbers must come from your aircraft and your risk assessment.</div>
