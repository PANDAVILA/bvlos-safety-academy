To execute the Safety Policy day to day, the Accountable Executive designates management personnel, typically a Director of Safety or Safety Manager. Part 5 requires these designated personnel to report regularly to the Accountable Executive. Now, pay close attention to organizational reporting lines.

Best practice is for the Safety Manager to have a direct line to the Accountable Executive, independent of operational line management such as the Chief Pilot or Director of Flight Operations. Why does this separation matter? Because Flight Operations is naturally driven by production targets: completing inspections on schedule, meeting client delivery windows, and maximizing flight hours. If the Safety Manager reports to the Chief Pilot, safety concerns can easily be overruled by commercial pressure. By reporting directly to the Accountable Executive, the Safety Manager keeps the independence to challenge and, if needed, halt unsafe operations without fear of reprisal.

At the sharp end of the operation, Remote Pilots in Command, Visual Observers, and Maintenance Technicians carry direct operational accountability. Every crew member should hold unconditional Stop-Work Authority, meaning any individual can pause or abort a launch if an unmitigated hazard is observed, with full backing from executive leadership.
