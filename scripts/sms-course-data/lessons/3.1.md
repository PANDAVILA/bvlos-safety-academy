## What you will learn

- The five steps of the SRM process
- The difference between a hazard and a consequence
- How to use the 5x5 risk matrix and the ALARP principle

## The five steps (AC 120-92D)

1. **System analysis**: describe airframe, GCS, C2 architecture, airspace, population, weather limits
2. **Hazard identification**: find conditions with the potential to cause harm
3. **Risk analysis**: estimate likelihood and the severity of the worst credible outcome
4. **Risk assessment**: plot on the matrix and compare with your acceptance criteria
5. **Risk control**: add barriers until the residual risk is acceptable and ALARP

**Hazard vs consequence.** Powerline electromagnetic interference is a hazard. Compass drift leading to a crash is the consequence.

**UAS hazard examples:** RF interference in urban canyons, GPS jamming or spoofing, battery voltage sag in cold weather, rotor icing, cognitive overload during multi-drone operations.

## The 5x5 matrix used in this course

| Zone | Meaning | Cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Unacceptable. No operation until mitigated | 3A, 2A, 2B, 1A, 1B, 1C |
| Yellow | Acceptable with mitigation and Accountable Executive sign-off | 4A, 4B, 3B, 3C, 2C, 2D, 1D, 1E |
| Green | Acceptable. Monitor routinely | all other cells |

Severity runs from 5 (negligible) to 1 (catastrophic). Likelihood runs from A (frequent) to E (extremely improbable).

<div class="callout"><strong>ALARP:</strong> zero risk exists only when the aircraft stays in its case. The goal is to drive residual risk As Low As Reasonably Practicable, weighing the severity of a ground impact against the cost and feasibility of mitigations. Your own organization defines its matrix and acceptance criteria. This one is a teaching example.</div>
