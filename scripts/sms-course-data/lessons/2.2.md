## What you will learn

- Safety roles and reporting lines in a UAS organization
- Why the Safety Manager should be independent of flight operations
- What Stop-Work Authority means in practice

## Roles

- **Accountable Executive**: final authority and accountability.
- **Director of Safety or Safety Manager**: runs the SMS day to day, tracks hazards, advises the AE. Part 5 requires designated management personnel who report regularly to the AE.
- **Chief Pilot or Director of Operations**: enforces SOPs and applies risk controls in daily flying.
- **RPIC, Visual Observers and maintenance staff**: pre-flight risk assessment, real-time hazard reporting, and adherence to procedures.

## Independence

Flight operations are driven by schedules and flight hours. If the Safety Manager reports to the Chief Pilot, safety concerns can be overruled by commercial pressure. Best practice is a direct line to the AE.

## Stop-Work Authority

Any crew member can pause or abort a launch when an unmitigated hazard is seen, and leadership backs that decision. Without that backing the authority is only on paper.
