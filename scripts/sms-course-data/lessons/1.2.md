## What you will learn

- How Reason's Swiss Cheese Model explains accident causation
- The difference between active failures and latent conditions
- How to trace a UAS accident back to organizational decisions

## The model

Think of your defenses as slices of Swiss cheese: policy, supervision, checklists, redundant systems. Every slice has holes that open, close and move. An accident happens when the holes line up and a hazard passes through every barrier.

The four layers used in this course follow the HFACS framework, which builds on Reason's work:

1. **Organizational influences**: budgets, quotas, weak SOPs, safety culture
2. **Unsafe supervision**: fatigued crews scheduled, maintenance intervals not enforced
3. **Preconditions for unsafe acts**: cold weather, degraded C2 link, firmware mismatch, fatigue
4. **Unsafe acts**: pilot input, improper battery handling, loss of visual line of sight

## Worked example

Management sets a powerline inspection quota with no budget for spare batteries. The Chief Pilot sees crews rushing turnarounds and does not intervene. Temperatures drop near freezing and the battery warning threshold is set too low. Under pressure to finish before sunset, the pilot commands a full-throttle climb, the battery voltage sags and the aircraft descends onto a road.

The pilot moved the stick, but the accident was set up months earlier. An SMS is designed to find and close those upstream holes.

<div class="callout"><strong>Key idea:</strong> if your investigation stops at "pilot error", you have only looked at the last slice of cheese.</div>
