## What you will learn

- What an Emergency Response Plan must cover
- The UAS-specific emergencies to plan for
- How to document and keep SMS records

## ERP basics

An ERP gives an orderly transition from normal to emergency operations and back. In the field, use the priority order **aviate, navigate, communicate, mitigate**. Every ERP needs:

- Who has authority in an emergency
- A notification tree (emergency services, ATC, NTSB, FAA, internal leadership)
- Immediate crew actions and evidence preservation
- A schedule of drills, with a debrief after each one

## UAS emergencies to plan for

- **Fly-away or loss of C2 link**: verify the failsafe, track last known position and energy, alert ATC if controlled airspace is threatened, use a flight termination system or parachute if public safety is at risk
- **Lithium battery thermal runaway**: people upwind and away, avoid the smoke, follow the manufacturer's suppression guidance, call the fire service if people or property are threatened
- **Mid-air or near mid-air collision**: emergency services first if anyone is hurt, then NTSB if the event meets the 49 CFR Part 830 definition of an accident, the FAA and your leadership. Preserve logs and wreckage.
- **Unauthorized entry to controlled airspace** and **injury or damage to third parties**

<div class="callout"><strong>Reporting duties are different things:</strong> NTSB notification (49 CFR Part 830) is immediate for accidents. Part 107 pilots also report to the FAA within 10 calendar days after serious injury, loss of consciousness or property damage above $500 (§107.9).</div>

## Documentation and records (curriculum 2.4)

- **SMS Manual**: the master document for policy, processes and organization
- **SRM records**: hazard analyses and the controls chosen
- **Retention under 14 CFR §5.97**: SRM outputs for as long as the control remains relevant; safety assurance outputs for at least 5 years; training records while the person is employed; safety communications for at least 24 consecutive months
