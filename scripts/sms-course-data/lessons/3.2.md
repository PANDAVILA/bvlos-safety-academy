## Hierarchy of Controls

From most to least reliable:

1. **Elimination**: cancel the flight in convective weather
2. **Substitution**: replace a single radio link with redundant C2 links on different technologies
3. **Engineering controls**: geofencing, obstacle sensing, parachute recovery systems (ASTM F3322 covers sUAS parachutes)
4. **Administrative controls**: SOPs, two-person challenge-and-response checklists, duty-time limits
5. **PPE**: eye protection, fire-resistant battery bags

Controls that depend on people remembering something are weaker than controls that work without them.

## JARUS SORA

The Specific Operations Risk Assessment combines the **Ground Risk Class** (risk to people on the ground) and the **Air Risk Class** (risk of meeting manned aircraft) to determine a **SAIL**, the Specific Assurance and Integrity Level. It is widely used by EASA and other authorities.

<div class="callout"><strong>Note for US operators:</strong> the FAA does not require SORA for Part 107 waivers or Part 135 approvals. The FAA uses its own process. SORA remains a useful way to structure the ground and air risk arguments in your safety case.</div>
