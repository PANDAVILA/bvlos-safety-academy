## What you will learn

- The four eras of aviation safety thinking and what each one added
- Why human factors matter at the Ground Control Station
- How the Total System Era connects manned traffic, UTM and data

## The four eras

| Era | Period | Main idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical | 1900s to 1960s | Fix the machine: structures, engines, instruments, airworthiness standards |
| Human Factors | 1970s to 1990s | Fix the interface: CRM, ergonomics, human-machine interface |
| Organizational | 1990s to 2000s | Fix the system: culture, management decisions and latent conditions |
| Total System | Today | Manage the whole ecosystem with data: manned and unmanned traffic, UTM, predictive analytics |

## What this means for UAS

- **Technical**: airframes, C2 links, batteries and software still fail, so reliability engineering remains essential.
- **Human factors**: a remote pilot works through a screen. Alert design, mode confusion and workload during multi-aircraft operations are safety issues, not user preferences.
- **Organizational**: a pilot who launches with a degraded battery is usually responding to pressure, weak procedures or poor oversight.
- **Total system**: BVLOS operations share airspace and data with other users, so safety cannot stop at your own fence.

## Check yourself

Think of your own operation. Which era does your current safety approach mostly reflect? What would it take to move to the next one?
