## What you will learn

- The roles of ICAO Annex 19 and Doc 9859
- Which operators Part 5 applies to, and what that means for Part 107
- Why a voluntary SMS still matters for waivers and clients

## The architecture

- **ICAO Annex 19**: requires states to run a State Safety Programme and to require an SMS from aviation service providers.
- **ICAO Doc 9859**: the Safety Management Manual, with the four-pillar framework and implementation guidance.
- **FAA 14 CFR Part 5**: the US SMS regulation. A 2024 final rule extended it to Part 135 certificate holders, §91.147 air tour operators and certain Part 21 certificate holders, with a 36-month phase-in for the newly covered operators. Part 121 was already covered.
- **FAA AC 120-92D** (May 2024): the FAA's guidance for building an SMS. It replaces AC 120-92B.
- **Related rules for UAS**: Part 107 (including waivers for BVLOS and operations over people), Part 89 (Remote ID) and Part 135 for drone delivery.

<div class="callout"><strong>Part 107 operators:</strong> Part 5 does not apply to Part 107 operations, so an SMS is voluntary. It is still one of the best ways to support a waiver application, an enterprise tender or an insurance review. Part 108, the proposed BVLOS rule, was still pending at the time this lesson was written. Check its current status.</div>

## Other frameworks you may meet

- **IS-BAO** (IBAC): a voluntary standard for business aviation operators, with an RPAS option.
- **EASA and JARUS**: SORA and the specific category for operations in Europe.
