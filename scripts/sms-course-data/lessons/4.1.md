## What you will learn

- How Safety Assurance differs from SRM
- Leading and lagging indicators, and how to set targets
- How hazard reporting systems and NASA ASRS work

## Safety Assurance

SRM designs the controls. Safety Assurance checks that they work in daily operations and detects **practical drift**, the gap between work-as-imagined and work-as-done.

## Indicators and targets

- **Lagging SPIs** measure past outcomes: fly-aways, hard landings, C2 link losses or airspace deviations per 1,000 flight hours
- **Leading SPIs** measure proactive health: briefing completion rate, hazard reports per month, battery health compliance
- **Targets (SPTs)** are measurable goals, for example "reduce uncommanded RTL events by 40% over the next quarter"

## Hazard reporting

Run a confidential, non-punitive internal reporting system, and close the loop with every reporter.

**NASA ASRS** accepts voluntary, confidential reports from UAS operators. Under FAA Advisory Circular 00-46F, a report filed within 10 days of an inadvertent event can waive civil penalty and certificate suspension, subject to the AC's conditions. Accidents and criminal acts are excluded.

<div class="callout"><strong>Do not confuse the duties:</strong> ASRS is voluntary. An accident that meets 49 CFR Part 830 must be reported to the NTSB immediately. Part 107 pilots must report certain injuries and damage to the FAA within 10 calendar days (§107.9).</div>
