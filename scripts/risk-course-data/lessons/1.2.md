## What you will learn

- The main regulatory pathways for BVLOS in the United States and the European Union
- Where SORA, PDRA and STS fit in the EU
- Where waivers fit in the US, and what the proposed Part 108 would change

## United States

- **Part 107 waiver**: BVLOS is not allowed under the standard §107.31 line-of-sight rule, so operators request a **waiver** and show how they achieve an equivalent level of safety. Waivers are requested in the FAA's **Aviation Safety Hub (ASHub)**. The FAA does not require SORA, but expects a CONOPS, a hazard analysis and clear mitigations.
- **Exemptions and other approvals**: heavier or more complex operations can need other approvals.
- **Proposed Part 108**: the FAA proposed a BVLOS rule in August 2025 with **permits** for lower-risk operations and **certificates** for higher-risk ones. At the time this course was written it was not a final rule. Treat it as a direction, not as a requirement.

## European Union

Regulation (EU) 2019/947 sets three categories: **open**, **specific** and **certified**. BVLOS normally sits in the **specific** category, where the operator must show the risk is acceptable. There are three routes:

- **STS (standard scenarios)**: a declaration against a defined scenario. STS-02 covers BVLOS over a controlled ground area in a sparsely populated area, with limits on distance and the use of airspace observers.
- **PDRA (predefined risk assessments)**: EASA has already done the risk assessment, for example **S-01, S-02, G-01, G-02 and G-03**. You prepare an Operations Manual and apply to your national authority, but the process is simplified.
- **SORA**: a full risk assessment, accepted as a means of compliance for Article 11. EASA and national authorities have been moving to **SORA 2.5**.

## Choosing a route

1. If a standard scenario or PDRA matches your operation, start there. It is usually faster.
2. If it does not match, use SORA. The extra effort is the evidence.
3. In the US, build the same ground-and-air argument and present it in the structure the FAA waiver form asks for.

<div class="callout"><strong>Check before you rely on it.</strong> PDRA and STS conditions (distance, population, aircraft size, airspace) are revised from time to time. Read the current text published by EASA and your national authority.</div>
