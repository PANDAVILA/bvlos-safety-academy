## What you will learn

- How containment limits the consequences of leaving the operational volume
- How the Operational Safety Objectives (OSOs) are organized
- Which types of evidence satisfy low, medium and high robustness

## Containment

The operational volume is a promise. Containment shows that, even if something fails, the UA stays inside the area you assessed or, if it leaves, you can show the risk is still acceptable. In SORA 2.5 the containment requirement is set at **low, medium or high** and depends on factors such as the maximum speed, the operational environment and the likelihood that the UA leaves the volume.

Ways to demonstrate containment include geofencing, independent termination or recovery systems, flight termination, reliable fail-safes and procedures that stop the flight before the boundary.

## Operational Safety Objectives

OSOs are the safety goals the operation must meet, grouped by area:

- **Technical issue with the UAS**: design, manufacturing, maintenance, software and hardware reliability
- **Deterioration of external systems**: C2 link, GNSS, external services and the services you contract
- **Human error**: crew training and competence, procedures, fatigue, crew coordination
- **Adverse operating conditions**: weather limits and the ability to detect them
- **Emergency response**: the Emergency Response Plan and the protection of involved persons

For each OSO the SAIL sets a required robustness: optional, low, medium or high. The numbering and some wording changed between SORA 2.0 and 2.5, so always use the annex that matches your version.

## Robustness = integrity + assurance

- **Integrity**: how good the mitigation is
- **Assurance**: how well you proved it

| Robustness | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Low | Declaration by the operator, supporting documents |
| Medium | Test results, procedures and training records the authority can review |
| High | Third-party validation or review, certification-grade evidence |
