- The difference between a hazard, a risk and a safety case
- The two ways a BVLOS flight can hurt someone: ground impact and mid-air collision
- Why BVLOS needs a structured argument, not just a checklist

## Hazard, risk and safety case

A **hazard** is a condition that can cause harm, for example a lost C2 link. **Risk** combines how severe the harm could be with how likely it is. A **safety case** is a structured argument, backed by evidence, that the residual risk of a specific operation is acceptable.

A table of risks is not a safety case. A safety case has three parts:

1. **Claim**: "This operation is acceptably safe to be flown in this volume under these limits."
2. **Argument**: why the claim holds, broken into ground risk, air risk, system reliability, people and procedures.
3. **Evidence**: test reports, manufacturer declarations, procedures, training records and data that support each part of the argument.

## Why VLOS reasoning breaks down in BVLOS

Within visual line of sight, the remote pilot is the main barrier: they see the traffic, see the people and can react. Beyond visual line of sight that barrier is gone, so the assessment must show what replaces it: technology, procedures, buffers or restrictions.

## The two main harm scenarios

- **Ground risk**: the unmanned aircraft (UA) hits a person or a vehicle on the surface. It depends on the UA mass and speed, the population below and the ways you can reduce impact or exposure.
- **Air risk**: the UA collides with a crewed aircraft. It depends on where you fly, how busy that airspace is and how well you can detect and avoid traffic.

Both feed one number that drives the rest of the dossier, the **SAIL** (Specific Assurance and Integrity Level), which you will derive in Module 4.

<div class="callout"><strong>Remember:</strong> a risk assessment answers "how risky is this operation?". A safety case answers "why should we believe that, and what happens when something fails?".</div>
