## What you will learn

- The structure of a safety case: claims, argument and evidence
- The ten SORA 2.5 steps, in order
- How to prepare an EASA specific category application

## The ten SORA 2.5 steps

1. **Documentation** of the proposed operation
2. **Ground risk**: adjacent area size and intrinsic GRC
3. **Final GRC** after ground mitigations
4. **Air risk**: initial ARC and adjacent airspace
5. **Strategic mitigations** and residual ARC
6. **TMPR**: the tactical mitigation performance requirement
7. **SAIL** determination
8. **Containment** requirements
9. **OSOs**: Operational Safety Objectives
10. **Comprehensive safety portfolio** and external services

Step 10 asks you to bring everything together and to document service level agreements for any external service you rely on, such as a C2 network provider or a weather service.

## Safety case structure

- **Top claim**: the operation is acceptably safe in its operational volume
- **Sub-claims**: ground risk is controlled, air risk is controlled, the UAS is reliable, the crew is competent, emergencies are handled
- **Evidence**: each sub-claim points to a document, a test report or a record
- **Assumptions and limits**: wind, visibility, density and traffic limits in which the claims are valid

## Preparing the application

For the EU specific category, the operator typically submits to the authority of the state of registration:

- The operational authorization application, with the SORA or the matching PDRA
- The **Operations Manual** with procedures that match the mitigations
- Evidence for each OSO at the robustness the SAIL requires
- Training and competence records, and the UAS technical information

Some authorities ask for a declaration of compliance for low SAILs and a review of evidence for higher ones. Ask early, because requirements differ by country.

<div class="callout"><strong>Traceability.</strong> Every mitigation claimed must appear in the Operations Manual, and every manual procedure that matters must trace back to a risk it controls. If a reviewer cannot find the link, they will assume it does not exist.</div>
