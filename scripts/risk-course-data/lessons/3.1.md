## What you will learn

- What the Air Risk Class (ARC) measures
- How to find the initial ARC from the airspace environment
- Why a low, rural, uncontrolled operation is not "no risk"

## What ARC measures

The ARC is a qualitative class, from **ARC-a** to **ARC-d**, for the chance that your UA meets a crewed aircraft. It is based on the airspace and environment you fly in, not on the UA.

| Class | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ARC-a | Atypical or segregated airspace. Very low encounter rate. |
| ARC-b | Low encounter rate |
| ARC-c | Medium encounter rate |
| ARC-d | High encounter rate |

## Finding the initial ARC (simplified flow)

1. **Atypical or segregated airspace** (for example a restricted or danger area where crewed traffic is excluded): ARC-a.
2. **Above FL 600**: ARC-b.
3. **Airport or heliport environment**: ARC-d in Class B, C or D airspace, otherwise ARC-c.
4. **Above 500 ft AGL**: ARC-d in a Mode-C veil, TMZ or controlled airspace, ARC-c in other airspace.
5. **At or below 500 ft AGL**: ARC-c in a Mode-C veil, TMZ, controlled airspace or urban areas, and **ARC-b in uncontrolled rural airspace**.

This course simplifies the flow so you can learn the logic. In a real dossier, follow the figure and airspace encounter categories in the SORA version your authority accepts, and justify the choice with airspace data.

## Common mistakes

- Using the lowest altitude of the route instead of the highest point of the operational volume
- Ignoring helicopter traffic, aerial work, gliders and paragliders at low level
- Treating Class G as empty. Crewed aircraft fly there too, often without transponders.
- Not including the contingency volume when you check the airspace

<div class="callout"><strong>Worked example.</strong> A rural pipeline inspection flown at 120 ft AGL in uncontrolled airspace, away from airports: initial ARC-b. The same route flown at 600 ft AGL becomes ARC-c.</div>
