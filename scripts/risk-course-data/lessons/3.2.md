## What you will learn

- Strategic mitigations that lower the encounter rate, and the residual ARC
- Tactical mitigation performance requirements (TMPR)
- What detect-and-avoid and airspace observers have to prove, in SORA and in FAA waivers

## Strategic mitigations

Strategic mitigations reduce how often an encounter could happen. They are applied **before** flight and are assessed with the authority:

- Operational restrictions by boundary, time of day or time of exposure
- Operating in or next to special use airspace, or other airspace requirements
- Pre-agreement with air navigation or UTM service providers
- NOTAM of the intended operation, and notification of military low-flying activity
- Outreach to local flying clubs and pilots

Some of these only work when someone independent accepts them. A NOTAM alone rarely lowers the class: crewed pilots may not read it and it does not stop a collision. Always present data, such as traffic counts.

## Residual ARC and TMPR

After strategic mitigations you have the **residual ARC**. It drives the **Tactical Mitigation Performance Requirement (TMPR)**, the performance the detect-and-avoid concept must reach:

| Residual ARC | TMPR |
| --- | --- |
| ARC-a | No requirement |
| ARC-b | Low |
| ARC-c | Medium |
| ARC-d | High |

## Detect and avoid in practice

Ways to meet a TMPR include airspace observers, ground-based radar, ADS-B or other cooperative sensors, onboard sensors and detect-and-avoid systems. Always treat **cooperative** and **non-cooperative** traffic separately. An average figure can hide poor performance against aircraft without transponders.

**FAA waiver view:** evaluators expect validation that grows with risk. A statement and data can be enough for low risk, third-party validation and an industry standard such as ASTM F3442 or RTCA DO-365 for medium risk, and certification for high risk. Ground radars need an FCC grant number in the application.

**Visual observers:** the FAA guidance gives reference detection distances of about 1.5 statute miles for traffic heading towards you and 2 to 2.5 on tangential tracks. If your total detect-and-avoid time is 30 seconds and the UA flies at 120 knots, you need 1 nautical mile. With a 1.5 NM detection distance, the maximum distance from the observer is about 0.5 NM.

<div class="callout"><strong>Remember:</strong> a mitigation counts only if you can show how you know it works. Plan the test and the data collection before you write the claim.</div>
