## What you will learn

- What the assessor needs to see in Step 1, the documentation of the operation
- The operational volume: flight geography, contingency volume and ground risk buffer
- What the adjacent area is and why it matters

## Step 1: describe the operation

Every later number depends on this description. Before you touch a table, write down:

- The **UAS**: type, maximum characteristic dimension, maximum speed, mass, and the safety features it really has
- The **mission**: purpose, route or area, altitude, day or night, duration
- The **environment**: terrain, population, airspace class and traffic, weather limits
- The **people and procedures**: crew roles, training, normal and contingency procedures
- The **C2 link and the detect-and-avoid concept** you intend to use

If the description is vague, the assessor will assume the worst case.

## The operational volume

The operational volume is built from three nested layers:

- **Flight geography (FG)**: where the UA is intended to fly in normal operation
- **Contingency volume (CV)**: the extra space the UA may use while you apply contingency procedures, such as a pilot-commanded return
- **Ground risk buffer (GRB)**: the ground area below and around the volume where a UA that has lost control could come down. It must be sized for how the UA fails: glide, ballistic descent or fly-away.

A common rule of thumb for the buffer is a **1:1 relationship between height and horizontal distance**, adjusted for speed, wind and failure mode. It is a starting point, not a substitute for analysis.

## The adjacent area

Even if the UA stays inside the volume, the assessor also looks at the **adjacent area** around it, because a UA that leaves the volume could end up there. In SORA 2.5 the adjacent area is sized from how far the UA could travel in a short, defined time after leaving the volume. The vertical limit uses the altitude the UA could gain in three minutes at maximum climb rate. Read the Main Body for the exact limits that apply to your version.

<div class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> draw it. A single map with FG, CV, GRB and the adjacent area, with the population density marked, makes the dossier much easier to review.</div>
