## What you will learn

- How to organize an FAA Part 107 BVLOS waiver package
- What waiver evaluators look for in a §107.31 request
- What the proposed Part 108 would change

## The waiver application

Part 107 waivers are requested through the FAA **Aviation Safety Hub (ASHub)**. The guide recommends a start date at least **90 days** after you apply, and the end date cannot be more than **48 months** after the start date. You complete a requested-waiver section for each rule and answer the **Waiver Safety Explanation Guidelines (WSEGs)**.

Upload a **Concept of Operations (CONOPS)** that describes the operation, the aircraft, the procedures, the location, the limitations, the hazards and the mitigations. Add maps, diagrams, procedures and safety material.

## What the evaluator reviews for §107.31

- How the remote pilot always knows the position, altitude and state of the UA and keeps it inside the intended area
- How the operation detects and avoids other aircraft, people and obstacles
- How the visual conspicuity of the UA is increased, and how the pilot is alerted to degraded functions
- Whether the crew has the knowledge, experience and skill for BVLOS
- The C2 links and their authorizations. Safety-critical links should not rely on equipment authorized only on an interference basis.

## The risk analysis

For each hazard, list severity and likelihood **before** and **after** mitigations, and the predicted residual risk. The FAA recommends mapping each mitigation to the exact place in your manual that implements it. The Responsible Person is accountable for everything submitted.

## Where SORA helps

The FAA does not require SORA, but the same ground-risk and air-risk work gives you the content: population exposure, ground buffers, detect-and-avoid performance and a documented residual risk.

## Proposed Part 108

The FAA proposed a BVLOS rule in August 2025, with **permits** for lower-risk operations and **certificates** for higher-risk ones, for aircraft up to 1,320 lb, generally at or below 400 ft AGL. Certificate holders would need a safety management system and a training program. Detect and avoid, anti-collision lighting and right-of-way rules would apply. In September 2026 the rule was still under White House review, so plan for waivers today and design your safety case so it can be reused.

<div class="callout"><strong>Check the status.</strong> The status of Part 108 can change quickly. Verify it on the FAA website before you tell a client what applies.</div>
